The short answer

AI automation uses language models such as OpenAI's GPT and Anthropic's Claude to handle repetitive work that needs some reading or judgment, like sorting emails, pulling data from documents or updating spreadsheets. Revonix designs and builds these automations end to end, connecting the AI step to your existing tools through Make or Zapier so the result lands where your team already works.

Key takeaways

  • AI automation handles tasks that need reading or light judgment, not only fixed rules.
  • OpenAI or Claude interprets the content; Make or Zapier moves the result into your tools.
  • Unclear cases go to a person, so the automation never guesses silently.
  • We start with one high-volume task and expand once it runs reliably.

What AI automation services cover

AI automation is software that does repetitive work which used to need a person to read something first. An email arrives, a PDF lands in a folder, a form gets submitted. Instead of someone opening it, working out what it says and typing the result into another system, a model like OpenAI's GPT or Claude reads it, decides what it is and passes clean, structured data to the next step.

Our AI automation services cover that full loop. The model is only one part. The rest is the plumbing that makes it dependable: triggers, validation checks, the handoff into your CRM or spreadsheet, and a clear route to a person when the model is not sure.

Signs your team needs AI automation

  • Someone spends the first hour of each day sorting a shared inbox.
  • Data gets retyped from emails, PDFs or web forms into a spreadsheet or CRM.
  • Requests sit unanswered because nobody clearly owns triage.
  • You tried rule-based automation, but the inputs vary too much for fixed rules.
  • Skilled staff lose hours to copy-and-paste work instead of the job they were hired for.

What we build

  • Inbox triage and routing

    Incoming email is read by Claude or OpenAI, labeled by intent and urgency, and routed to the right person or queue. For common requests, the system can prepare a draft reply that a person checks and sends.

  • Document and data extraction

    Invoices, order forms, applications and contracts are read and turned into named fields. Values are checked against rules you define, such as required fields or totals that must add up, before anything is written to your systems.

  • Spreadsheet and CRM updates

    Make or Zapier take the extracted data and write it where it belongs: a row in your spreadsheet, a contact or deal in HubSpot, a task for the right owner. Your team keeps working in the tools it knows.

  • Summaries and reporting

    Long threads, call notes and weekly activity are condensed into short summaries and posted where your team already reads, so nobody has to scroll back through forty messages to catch up.

  • Review lists for edge cases

    Anything the model flags as uncertain goes to a simple review step. A person confirms or corrects it in a few clicks, and the automation carries on from there.

Example: maintenance requests for a property management company

An illustrative example of a typical project, not a client case study.

A property management company with a few hundred units receives maintenance requests by email. Tenants write in a hurry, the unit number is buried somewhere in the text, and photos are attached. An office coordinator reads each one, works out the address and urgency, and logs it in a shared spreadsheet before calling a contractor.

We would take over the reading and logging and leave the coordinator with the judgment calls: which contractor to send and how to talk to the tenant.

What we would build

  • An email trigger in Make that picks up each new request.
  • A Claude step that extracts the unit, issue type and urgency from free text.
  • A new row in the maintenance spreadsheet with a link back to the original email.
  • An instant alert for anything flagged as urgent, such as a leak or no heat.
  • A review list for requests the model could not match to a unit.

What kinds of tasks can AI automation handle?+
Tasks where someone currently reads something and then types or files the result. Common examples are sorting a shared inbox, extracting fields from invoices or forms, summarizing long email threads and updating a CRM or spreadsheet from free text. If the input varies too much for fixed rules but the decision is routine, it is usually a good fit.
How is AI automation different from regular workflow automation?+
Regular workflow automation moves data between tools when a fixed condition is met. AI automation adds a step that understands unstructured input, such as an email written in plain language, and turns it into structured data or a decision. Most of our AI automations run on top of a normal Make or Zapier workflow.
What happens when the AI gets something wrong?+
We design for it. Each automation checks extracted values against rules you set, and the model reports when it is unsure. Anything that fails a check or falls below a confidence threshold goes to a review list instead of straight into your systems, and we use those cases to improve the prompts.
Do we need to replace our current tools?+
No. The point is to work with the inbox, spreadsheets, CRM and accounting software you already run. Make and Zapier connect to thousands of common business apps, so the AI step simply sits between the tools you have.
How much data do we need before starting?+
Far less than people expect. A few dozen real examples of the emails or documents you handle are usually enough to build and test the first version. We use them to measure accuracy before anything goes live.
