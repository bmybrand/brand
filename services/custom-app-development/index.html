The short answer

Custom web and mobile app development means building software around your own workflows and users instead of bending an off-the-shelf product to fit. Revonix builds web apps in React and Next.js, back ends in Node.js or Python and Django, and mobile apps in Flutter, hosted on AWS, often as the interface that makes an AI system usable day to day.

Key takeaways

  • The app is shaped around your users and process, not a generic template.
  • We pick the back end, Node.js or Python and Django, to match the work, especially where AI is involved.
  • Flutter lets one codebase serve both iOS and Android.
  • You own the code, the accounts and the hosting on AWS.

What custom web and mobile app development means

A custom app is software built for one specific job and one specific group of users. It might be a customer portal, an app your field staff use on site, or the front end of an AI system that would otherwise live in scripts and spreadsheets. Because it is built for your process, nobody has to work around features they do not need or missing ones they do.

That matters most when AI is involved. A model that classifies documents or drafts replies is only useful if people can see its output, correct it and act on it in a clear interface. Our custom web app development and mobile work turns those systems into tools people actually open every day.

Who it's for

  • Your process does not fit any off-the-shelf product without awkward workarounds.
  • You have built an AI workflow and now need a proper interface for staff or customers.
  • Customers have to email or phone for information they could check themselves.
  • Field teams fill in paper forms that someone retypes later.
  • An existing app has become slow, fragile or hard to change.

What we build

  • Web applications in React and Next.js

    Fast, accessible web apps for staff and customers, from portals and dashboards to full products. Next.js gives quick page loads and good search visibility for any public-facing parts.

  • Back ends in Node.js or Python and Django

    APIs, business logic, user accounts and integrations. We choose Node.js for real-time and integration-heavy work and Python with Django where data processing or AI libraries do most of the lifting.

  • Mobile apps in Flutter

    iOS and Android apps from one codebase, with offline support, camera, location and push notifications where your users need them.

  • AI features inside the app

    Document upload with automatic extraction, assistants that answer questions inside the product, smart search and suggested actions, built on OpenAI or Claude behind your own back end.

  • Hosting and deployment on AWS

    Production infrastructure with separate test environments, automated deployments, backups and monitoring, set up in your own AWS account.

How a project runs

  1. 1

    Discovery

    We meet the people who will use the app, walk through the work it needs to support and identify the one or two screens that matter most. Those become the core of the first release.
  2. 2

    Strategy

    We choose the stack for this app, web, mobile or both, and plan the data model, integrations and release order. The plan, timeline and price are agreed before building.
  3. 3

    Delivery

    We ship working builds in short cycles, starting with the core screens, so your users can try the app on real tasks while the rest is still in progress.
  4. 4

    After launch we monitor errors and performance, ship updates and security patches, keep app store listings current and build new features based on how people use the app.

Example: a site inspection app for a commercial cleaning contractor

An illustrative example of a typical project, not a client case study.

A commercial cleaning contractor sends supervisors to check client sites every week. They fill in paper checklists, take photos on their own phones and email them to the office, where someone compiles a report for each client. Reports go out late, photos get lost and clients have little visibility between visits.

We would replace the paper and email loop with a mobile app for supervisors and a web portal for the office and clients.

What we would build

  • A Flutter app with site checklists, photo capture and offline saving for basements and parking garages.
  • A Django back end that stores inspections and flags issues that need follow-up.
  • An AI step that turns checklist notes and photos into a short written summary for each visit.
  • A Next.js portal where clients see their inspection history and open issues.
  • Hosting on AWS with role-based access for supervisors, office staff and clients.

Should we build a web app or a mobile app first?+
Start where your users are. Office teams and most business customers do well with a web app, which is faster to update and works on any device with a browser. Field staff, frequent on-the-go use, offline access or camera and location features point toward a mobile app.
Why do you use Flutter for mobile apps?+
Flutter builds iOS and Android apps from one codebase, so features ship to both platforms together and there is less code to maintain. It produces fast, native-feeling interfaces and suits the business apps we build. For a few cases that need deep platform-specific features, we discuss alternatives during planning.
Who owns the code?+
You do. The code lives in your repository, and the hosting, app store and third-party accounts are set up in your business's name. We document how it is built and deployed, so another team could take it over if needed.
Can you add AI features to an app we already have?+
Often, yes. We review the existing code and architecture first. If the app is in good shape, we add AI features through new back-end services that call OpenAI or Claude. If it is not, we explain the options plainly, including when a partial rebuild is the better route.
How do you handle hosting and security?+
We host on AWS with separate environments for testing and production, encrypted connections, managed databases with backups and access limited to the people who need it. Authentication, user roles and audit logs are planned in from the start rather than added later.
